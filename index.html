Auto Sync

Live stats line up with your stream by reading the scoreboard on your screen. Nothing to press.

  1. It takes a frame. About once a second, the app grabs the frame on screen from whichever player engine is running.
  2. It finds the scoreboard. A corner scoreboard can be a few percent of the picture, too small to read in one look at the whole frame. Until it finds a clock, the app reads the top and bottom thirds in turn, zoomed in, and the whole frame every third time. Once found, it reads only a band across the clock's line. Baseball finds its scoreboard by the line naming a team.
  3. It reads the scoreboard. Apple's text recognition reads the broadcast's clock and score on the device itself. Nothing is uploaded.
  4. It compares with ESPN. ESPN records the real time of every play, with the game clock and score at that moment. When your screen first shows 4:52 and ESPN logged a play at 4:52 thirty seconds ago, your stream is 30 seconds behind.
  5. It waits for late plays. ESPN posts some plays minutes after they happen. In one NHL game a hit reached the feed 208 seconds late, so a stream only a little behind showed it first. Every clock and score the app sees is kept for ten minutes and checked again as plays arrive, so it still locks.
  6. It follows each sport's clock. Football and basketball count down, as ESPN does. Hockey broadcasts count down while ESPN counts up within the period, so the app converts. Soccer's clock never stops, so every reading gives an answer, which is why soccer locks fastest. Baseball has no clock, so it waits for the score to change.
  7. It throws out bad readings. A misread digit or a clock frozen at 45:00 in stoppage time can mislead. The app waits until several estimates agree within 2.5 seconds, then adds a little on the late side so stats never run ahead of the picture.
  8. It keeps watching. Once locked it checks every 4 seconds in case the stream reconnects. It rests when the device runs hot or the app is in the background, and it can be switched off where you sync by hand.
  9. It says what it sees. The sync line says how far it has got: no picture from the player, looking for the scoreboard, or the clock it reads and how many plays agree.

Live stats while you watch

A Stats button on the player opens the game's score, win probability, play-by-play, box score and leaders. On iPhone they sit under the picture upright, and beside it turned sideways. On Apple TV they sit beside the picture, which narrows to make room.

Source
ESPN's game feed, every 10 seconds. NFL, college football, NBA, NHL and MLB.
No spoilers
ESPN stamps each play with the moment it happened. A play shows only once that moment plus your stream's delay has passed. The box score waits for the latest play that came with it, and the game's score on the Sports tab is held the same way.
Pausing
Holds everything back by as long as you paused.
Sync by hand
The sync steps show the latest play and ask if it's on your screen: It Just Happened, Not Yet or Already Saw It. Until a sync, everything is held a minute.
On Apple TV
Stats is a button in the full-screen controls. Every row takes focus, so the lists scroll. Select on a play syncs to it, and the sync chip opens the sync steps inside the panel. Back leaves the steps, then closes the panel. It stays open from one game to the next.

Soccer, tennis, UFC and racing stats

The same stats panel for four more sports, with the same rule: nothing shows before your picture does.

Soccer
ESPN's match commentary logs every shot, corner, card and substitution with its moment. Goals carry no running score, so the app counts it from the commentary.
Scoreboards
For tennis, UFC, F1 and NASCAR, ESPN only has a scoreboard. The app compares each scoreboard with the last one to work out what happened in between (a break of serve, a takedown, a new leader) and times each change between the two fetches, so it never shows early.
Tennis
The match with the server marked, and a Courts tab with every court of the day. Other courts update live. Only the match on your screen is held.
UFC
The fight on now and the whole card, with results and how each fight ended.
Racing
The session, the lap and the running order, with places gained and lost.

Collections

Your own collections of movies and shows. On iPhone they're on a Collections shelf. On Apple TV they're under Collections in the Library menu.

Making them
Create, rename and delete. Add a title by touching and holding its poster, or with the collection button on its page. Each card previews up to three posters.
Making them
Hold Select on a movie or show and pick Collections. Select adds it to or takes it out of each one, and a new one is named with the TV's keyboard. Hold Select on a collection to rename or delete it.
Server ones
Your media servers' own collections are listed too, to browse. They aren't changed.
Storage
On the device. Each title is saved with its server, so the same title on two servers stays separate.
Order
Move Collections like any other shelf in Edit Shelves.

Performance sweep

Less work on every screen, every refresh and every launch.

Redrawing
The app moved to Swift's newer change tracking (Observation), so a screen redraws only when what it shows changes.
Guide
The app asks your provider whether the guide changed, and skips the download if it didn't.
Opening
The schedule is saved to a file, so the app opens with it already filled in.
Re-checks
Refreshes where only scores changed re-check channels at most every 5 minutes.
Heat
In Multiview, when the phone runs hot, the muted picture drops its quality.
Reports
On iPhone, a Device Reports screen in Settings shows Apple's own performance reports for the app.
