Auto Sync

Live stats line up with your stream by reading the scoreboard on your screen. No taps.

  1. It takes a frame. About once a second, the app grabs the frame on screen from whichever player engine is running.
  2. It reads the scoreboard. Apple's text recognition reads the broadcast's clock and score on the iPhone itself. Nothing is uploaded.
  3. It compares with ESPN. ESPN records the real time of every play, with the game clock and score at that moment. When your screen first shows 4:52 and ESPN logged a play at 4:52 thirty seconds ago, your stream is 30 seconds behind.
  4. It follows each sport's clock. Football and basketball count down, as ESPN does. Hockey broadcasts count down while ESPN counts up within the period, so the app converts. Soccer's clock never stops, so every reading gives an answer, which is why soccer locks fastest. Baseball has no clock, so it waits for the score to change.
  5. It throws out bad readings. A misread digit or a clock frozen at 45:00 in stoppage time can mislead. The app waits until several estimates agree within 2.5 seconds, then adds a little on the late side so stats never run ahead of the picture.
  6. It keeps watching. Once locked it checks every 4 seconds in case the stream reconnects. It rests when the phone runs hot or the app is in the background, and it can be switched off in the sync sheet.

Live stats while you watch

A Stats button on the player opens the game's score, win probability, play-by-play, box score and leaders. Upright they sit under the picture. Turned sideways, beside it.

Source
ESPN's game feed, every 10 seconds. NFL, college football, NBA, NHL and MLB.
No spoilers
ESPN stamps each play with the moment it happened. A play shows only once that moment plus your stream's delay has passed. The box score waits for the latest play that came with it, and the game's score on the Sports tab is held the same way.
Pausing
Holds everything back by as long as you paused.
Sync by hand
The sync sheet shows the latest play and asks if it's on your screen: It Just Happened, Not Yet or Already Saw It. Until a sync, everything is held a minute.

Soccer, tennis, UFC and racing stats

The same stats panel for four more sports, with the same rule: nothing shows before your picture does.

Soccer
ESPN's match commentary logs every shot, corner, card and substitution with its moment. Goals carry no running score, so the app counts it from the commentary.
Scoreboards
For tennis, UFC, F1 and NASCAR, ESPN only has a scoreboard. The app compares each scoreboard with the last one to work out what happened in between (a break of serve, a takedown, a new leader) and times each change between the two fetches, so it never shows early.
Tennis
The match with the server marked, and a Courts tab with every court of the day. Other courts update live. Only the match on your screen is held.
UFC
The fight on now and the whole card, with results and how each fight ended.
Racing
The session, the lap and the running order, with places gained and lost.

Performance sweep

Less work on every screen, every refresh and every launch.

Redrawing
The app moved to Swift's newer change tracking (Observation), so a screen redraws only when what it shows changes.
Guide
The app asks your provider whether the guide changed, and skips the download if it didn't.
Opening
The schedule is saved to a file, so the app opens with it already filled in.
Re-checks
Refreshes where only scores changed re-check channels at most every 5 minutes.
Heat
In Multiview, when the phone runs hot, the muted picture drops its quality.
Reports
On iPhone, a Device Reports screen in Settings shows Apple's own performance reports for the app.
